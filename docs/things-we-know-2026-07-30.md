# Things we know (2026-07-30)

One flat list, no buckets. Sorted by confidence × importance — well-established and consequential at the top, speculative or minor at the bottom. Tags: [confidence · importance]. Sibling of `learnings-2026-07-30.md` (Nathan's markup lives there); numbers cite this week's analyses + the stats dashboard.

1. **X decides how many notes per day we're allowed to post, based on our recent hit rate — and we hit that wall every day.** (Rewritten from jargon per Nathan's note.) The allowance formula is public; fed our live data it reproduced X's actual cap on two occasions, so we trust its shape. In our current band, each +1 percentage point of net hit rate (helpfuls minus not-helpfuls, over ALL recent notes written) buys roughly 3 more notes/day of allowance. Writing more notes does nothing to the allowance right now; only the hit rate moves it. [high · high]

2. **~85% of our notes never receive a single rating**, and unrated notes still count in every hit-rate denominator X uses (max of last-100 / 14d / lifetime — all total-written). Net HR = rated-at-all (~0.13) × quality-if-rated (~0.33). [high · high]
3. **Feed tier degrades net quality monotonically: small +9.0% (n=3224) > large +6.7% (n=1727) > xl +4.0% (n=273) > xxl −3.5% (n=144)** (all-time, dashboard). Broader feeds make more helpful AND more unhelpful, at worsening ratio. (Nathan: "we need some kind of view on how to do this better? Like a frame?" → lemma 27.) ⚠ **DOES NOT REPRODUCE (2026-08-24)** — on 706 notes Aug 7–17 with 7-day labels, at matched velocity (≥15k/h) the order is **reversed**: xl +10.1% (n=298) > large +8.5% (n=200) > small +4.2% (n=48). Note also only ~72 small-tier posts/day are surfaced now (2.2% of feed), so "small" is a thin slice. Needs a proper re-measure before anyone leans on this to argue against widening. [was high · now contested]
4. **Within a session, top-of-sort submissions do much better than deep ones** (~80% vs ~50% helpful-of-rated, deep tail ~net-negative). Coarse cut solid; rank-1-vs-7 precision is overreading. [high · high]
5. **Eval score is the only live ranking signal: AUC 0.67, monotone** (<0 ≈ +2% · [0,0.5) ≈ +5% · [0.5,1) ≈ +7% · ≥1 ≈ +13–15% net). Plainly: eval predicts how a note does IF people rate it; it does not predict WHETHER anyone rates it — so ranking by eval can raise quality-of-rated but cannot raise the ~15% of notes that get seen at all. [high · high]

6. **Arm-level A/Bs on helpful-status take months at ~2–3 rated notes/day.** The fast loops are counterfactual backtests, process metrics (abstention, submits/day), and Nathan's tags. [high · high — method]



7. **The biggest NH bucket is relevance/engagement, not factual error**: did-not-engage + pedantic + didn't-convince ≈ 43% of lifetime NH; Nathan's tags on these are ~100% predictive of not-helpful. The materiality judge aimed at it remains unbuilt. [high · high]
8. **Raters don't count time-bound tweets as wrong** (Nathan's model: humans distinguish "right at the time, wrong now" from "always wrong"; the writer couldn't). Breaking-news cluster 0H/15NH; time-travel backtest flags 9NH vs ~3H at 4.7% FP. Fix live as #323, read ~mid-Aug. [high on the failure · med on the fix's live effect]
9. **Economics: ~$1.05/written note, ~$18/helpful note, ~$0.15 per 1k raw views, ~$0.24–0.27 per 1k importance-weighted views.** Cheap vs any benchmark; marginal scaling cost ~flat so far; Nathan: could spend up to double easily. View data stale (manual scrape, ~8wk). [med-high · high]
10. **Notes on tweets >24h old are near-worthless** (6.7% ever rated, +1.5% net, n=310); the 3–12h band is the sweet spot; <3h is slightly *worse* than 3–12h. [high · med]
11. **Posting is jagged: 44% of active sessions submit zero; mean ~1.1/session; ~4 in 10 scheduled runs process nothing** (wall already hit). [high · med]
12. **Trivia is 36% of our helpful notes and 37% of view-mass** — no slop-skew in views. And per Nathan: we don't actually know how to trade off important vs trivial views — open values question, not a data question. [med-high · med]
13. **Nothing predicts rated-at-all today** — eval is flat on it, no other live signal tries. Nathan's markup promotes "predict this" to a build candidate. **Confirmed by the Aug 22 predictor run** (`tmp/2026_08_20_predictor/train.py`, n=5,194): every model sits at chance out-of-time — eval-only temporal AUC 0.518, best fitted 0.512, all-features 0.525. Same run found **eval-only BEATS all 46-feature models out-of-time** on helpful (0.577 vs ≤0.575) and not-helpful (0.663 vs ≤0.570) — the fitted models win on CV and lose temporally, i.e. they overfit a moving pipeline. Two caveats: velocity was missing for 89% of those rows (archiving only began Aug 7), and the "PRE" feature set included the eval score, so **admission-time-only prediction has never been fitted** — that is the untested model, and the one lemma 27 needs. Lemmas — inferences, kept separate from the facts per Nathan's note:

25. **Lemma (from 1, 4, 5):** a quality floor on marginal submissions should raise the allowance — deep-in-session submissions run near or below the portfolio's average hit rate, and every below-average note shrinks tomorrow's cap. The flat-0.5 version and the stale cutoff (from 10) are the shippable forms.
26. **Lemma (from 2, 13, 15):** predicting and targeting *rated-at-all* is the largest untested lever — the ~0.13 coverage factor caps everything, no live signal addresses it, and the one measured lead (note-requests) is idle.
28. **The fast lane (≥30k impressions/hr) is ~4% of the eligible feed and ~a quarter of it is notable** (checkable + suspect): 800-tweet classified sample of the Jul 18 full-feed capture → ~311 notable posts per snapshot vs our ~40/day cap and 6-of-1,221 noted. Supply is abundant; selection among abundance is the game. Composition: politics 24% of lane (42% notable — biggest pool, worst bridging), sports 22%, celebrity 12%, AI-slop only 3% of lane but **74% notable** (precision target; those notes also rate well + carry views), health+scam ~4% combined — **the important-view classes barely surface at high velocity**; hunting them needs the low-floor topic machinery. Velocity-chasing and importance-chasing are partially opposed, now with numbers. [med-high · high] (`tmp/fastlane_*`, 2026-08-03)

29. **Dataset infrastructure for fast-lane work exists — and since Aug 7 it is continuous:** every ladder-surfaced post is archived into `feed_tweets` with impressions and tier frozen at first sight (commit 2341075); 85,350 rows by Aug 22, up from one Jul 18 capture of 29,412. **95% of notes written since Aug 7 have fetch-time velocity**; earlier notes have almost none, so the gap is timing, not capture failure. Velocity-complete labels accrue ~700/week. The manual `capture-feed-tweets` workflow still exists for full-feed snapshots. [high · med]

30. **A note's status is final by day 7 — adopted readout convention (Nathan, 2026-08-24).** On 1,061 notes watched from submission with 30-day follow-up, day-7 status equals final status for **1010/1015 (99.5%)**; the five exceptions were 2 NH→NMR, 2 NMR→H, 1 NMR→NH. Helpful verdicts reach 97% of their final count by day 3 and 100% by day 4; not-helpful lands faster (85% by day 1) but **overshoots ~25%** (49 ever-NH vs 39 final) and partly reverts, so a day-1 NH reading is the noisiest signal we have. Sharpens item 6: the A/B wait is rated-note *accumulation*, not maturation — a cohort is readable 7 days after its last note, and labels for training are available 3× sooner than the old 30-day habit allowed. Caveats: status only (views not measured), and the scrape grid is dense to ~day 8 then silent until 30d+, so day-7 is the anchor observation rather than day-14. (`tmp/2026_08_20_predictor/convergence.ts` + `day7_agreement.ts`) [high · med-high]

31. **We are quality-bound, not volume-bound, and the official formula underpredicts our real cap ~2.4×.** The daily probe (PR #370) reported `Binding constraint: QUALITY (WL_L)` on all 8 readings Aug 17–24, with the volume term (DN_30×5 ≈ 314) about 6× the quality term (WL_L 37.7→47.9). So writing *more* notes does not raise the cap; only hit rate does. But the formula's arithmetic is not trustworthy in level: on Aug 17–20 we posted 87/110/80/91 against predicted caps of 37/38/41/42 **with no refusal**, and the last actual 403 was Aug 14 at 90. HR_L climbed 0.0825→0.0970 over the window (~+0.2pp/day). ⚠ The formula has a kink at HR_L 0.10 where the slope goes 700→3000, but since the level is off by 2.4× the kink's location should not be trusted. `pipeline_state.writing_limit` (116) is a ratchet guess, not an observation, and is not enforced — rationing auto-disables after 12h without a 403. [high · high]

32. **The velocity floor was the binding constraint on supply, not our processing capacity.** We surface ~3,285 posts/day into `feed_tweets`; only ~841 cleared the 15k/h floor; we processed ~900 — i.e. essentially everything the floor permitted. The per-run cap of 20 is NOT binding (median run 11, p90 18, only 6–7% of runs reach 20), and the request side isn't either (`FEED_MAX_POSTS = 50_000`, `FEED_MAX_PAGES = 500`). Floor sensitivity, posts/day: 30k→519 · 15k→841 · 10k→1,028 · **5k→1,392** · 2k→1,870 · 0→3,285. Surfaced tier mix is xl 2,320/day (70.6%) · large 897 (27.3%) · small 72 (2.2%). Two knobs remain unexplored: we fetch small/large/xl but **not xxl**, and **English only** (`feed_lang: en`). Acted on: floor → 5k (PR #390). [high · high]

33. **Selection cannot happen at submission — it has to happen at admission.** `submitCandidates` does not re-sort ("Nothing is re-sorted here"); pipeline order is tier then **velocity descending**, which the item-23 supersede suggests is backwards. But sorting there is moot today: we submit essentially everything that survives the funnel (Aug 20: 966 processed → 578 prefilter → 122 no-correction → 73 check-failed → 91 submitted) and limit-skipped has been 0 since Aug 15, so there is no queue to reorder. More importantly the eval score arrives *after* search, writer and verifier are paid for, so it can never guide spend. Every admitted post costs a full pipeline run against a submission ceiling of ~115–120. **The missing piece is an expected-net-helpful score on fetch-time features only** (tier, velocity, author, text) — never fitted, because the Aug 22 run's "PRE" set included eval. That is lemma 27's real form. [high · high]

27. **Lemma (from 3, 23, 23b) — the frame Nathan asked for on item 3:** admission should be ONE policy — estimate each candidate tweet's expected net-helpful contribution from tier × velocity × eval jointly, admit above a single bar — instead of today's three uncoordinated filters (tier ladder, velocity floor, eval gate). The fit MUST include rated-at-all as a function of velocity (23b shows coverage, not quality-of-rated, is where velocity pays); with that term the optimal floor may sit at or above Jim's 30k, not below it as the raw helpful-supply CDF suggests.
