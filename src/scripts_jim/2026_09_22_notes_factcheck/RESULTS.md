### Contra Benn Jordan, data center (and all) sub-audible infrasound issues are fake
- Link: https://commonnotes.net/?project=andymasley&note=83277e70-69ea-42ba-834d-1f28d5cfdacb
- Post: https://blog.andymasley.com/p/contra-benn-jordan-data-center-and
- Whose words: creator
**The passage:** I can't find any paper by Armand & Bikaran 2025 on infrasound, PIEZO1, or anything like it. If anyone reading this can find it, let me know, but as it stands I'm going to treat it as made up until proven otherwise.
**The note:** A real, indexed paper exists: Armand & Bikaran et al., "The Role of Infrasound and Audible Acoustic Sound in Modulating Wound Healing," International Wound Journal 2025;22(5):e70243, doi:10.1111/iwj.70243. It covers infrasound and wound healing, not PIEZO1.
**Why:** This is Andy in his own voice, replying to a block quote from Benn Jordan's rebuttal that cites "[Armand & Bikaran, 2025; MDPI, 2026]" for the claim that low-frequency noise can modulate PIEZO1 channels: "I can't find any paper by Armand & Bikaran 2025 on infrasound, PIEZO1, or anything like it. If anyone reading this can find it, let me know, but as it stands I'm going to treat it as made up until proven otherwise." The live post still says this, unchanged. The paper exists and turns up on the first search for the two names plus infrasound: Crossref and Europe PMC both list "The Role of Infrasound and Audible Acoustic Sound in Modulating Wound Healing: A Systematic Review" by Aryna C. Armand, Matin Bikaran, Timothy B. Gardner and Michael K. Matthew, International Wound Journal volume 22 issue 5, article e70243, doi 10.1111/iwj.70243, published 27 April 2025, PubMed 40288769, PMC12034371, no retraction. It is a review of infrasound at 1 to 20 Hz and low-frequency sound on wound and bone healing, and the open-access full text does not contain the word PIEZO at all, so the note's last sentence is also right and keeps Andy's real point that the citation does not support what Jordan used it for. Calling a real citation made up is the harshest accusation in a very long post, so a reader who believes it comes away with a wrong belief about Jordan that matters. Andy asked readers to tell him if they could find it, so he should welcome this. Nothing in the note needs changing.

### AI researchers debate how close we are to recursive self-improvement
- Link: https://commonnotes.net/?project=dwarkesh&note=5eb42f60-e9d5-436a-b930-069b5d8fcd86
- Post: https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/john-beren-charlie
- Whose words: creator
**The passage:** Because it was never initialized on human data, it can think in ways that humans are not even thinking and come up with extremely creative solutions.
**The note:** The AlphaGo version that played move 37 against Lee Sedol in March 2016 was initially trained on human games. DeepMind says it was introduced to thousands of expert games so the system could learn how humans play before reinforcement learning.
**Why:** Dwarkesh says this in his own voice while framing a question: "AlphaGo coming up with move 37, the super creative move. Because it was never initialized on human data, it can think in ways that humans are not even thinking." He then asks about "de novo initialization of intelligence", so the premise carries the question, and nobody on the panel corrects it; Beren only remarks that AlphaGo used MCTS. The premise is wrong. **The note:** Little Boy (~15 kt) isn't one of the smallest nukes ever developed; it's small only vs megaton-class weapons. The US W54 warhead had yields of just 10-20 tons of TNT, about 1,000 times smaller than Little Boy, and was the smallest US nuclear weapon fielded.
**Why:** The sentence is Andy's own and he leans on it: he computes 14.3 kilotons, writes "This is about the same as the Little Boy bomb dropped on Hiroshima. While horrible, this is also one of the smallest nuclear weapons ever developed", and earlier says "the specific nuclear bomb people are using for this comparison is among the smallest ever made" as part of his argument that the reporters picked an alarming unit. The live post still says this with no correction. So Little Boy is roughly a thousand times larger than the smallest fielded weapon, and the sentence is wrong as written. It is not a pure nitpick, because a reader takes away that the journalists reached for one of the smallest bombs ever built to inflate their count, and that is false; but the error does not damage his real point, which is that yields span five orders of magnitude so the unit is meaningless, and the true facts only strengthen it. He may therefore read the note as correcting a throwaway line. One flaw in the note itself: "it's small only vs megaton-class weapons" overreaches, since Little Boy is also small next to the ordinary strategic warheads of 90 to 500 kilotons he shows in his own arsenal image.

**Before sending:** Drop the clause "it's small only vs megaton-class weapons" and say instead that Little Boy is small next to modern strategic warheads but far from the smallest ever built; keep the W54 comparison as the correction.
