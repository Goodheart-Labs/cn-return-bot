# Failure mode: unhelpful_missing_key_points

This failure mode covers notes that state something true but do not address the tweet's actual misleading argument. The tweet or embedded media makes a specific claim — often invoking a conspiracy framing, a false attribution, or an implied narrative — and the note answers a different, easier question while leaving the real damage intact. The judge should reject any note that, no matter how accurate, allows the tweet's central false implication to survive unchallenged.

---

### Note rebuts a peripheral detail, ignores the main conspiracy claim

**Tweet:** Was a live child auction conducted during the 2014 Oscars? "They went really overboard with the pizza obsession, which I feel like makes it more suspicious."  
[media: video overlaid with "PIZZAGATE CONFIRMED DISTURBING 2014 OSCARS"]

**Note (REJECT):** The video misidentifies Hollywood producer Jeffrey Katzenberg as Jeffrey Epstein. The 2014 Oscars pizza segment was a comedic bit where real pizza was delivered and eaten by attendees, not a child auction. [sources]

**Why reject:** The central claim is a Pizzagate conspiracy; fixing a name mix-up does not rebut it. Reject notes that address a peripheral factual error while leaving the tweet's core conspiratorial assertion — that the event was suspicious or involved child trafficking — completely unaddressed.

### Note corrects the wrong misattribution in footage

**Tweet:** The train to Auschwitz, 1942. This footage, in color, shattered my heart. So innocent. So noble. At the final station, their lives vanished — dressed in their modest finery, moving toward the gas chambers.  
[media: historical deportation footage labeled "KAMP WESTERBORK"]

**Note (REJECT):** This footage is not original 1942 color film. The Nazis filmed in black and white only. Any color footage is modern digital colorization of archival footage, not authentic 1942 filming. [source]

**Why reject:** The tweet misattributes both the location (Westerbork, not Auschwitz) and the date (1944, not 1942) — but the note only addresses colorization. Reject notes that correct a secondary technical detail while the primary misattribution — wrong camp, wrong year — remains unaddressed and the misleading emotional framing stands.

[media: photo of undamaged KC-135s at Sioux City Air National Guard Base, Iowa]

**Note (REJECT):** The image shows undamaged KC-135 refueling planes at Sioux City Air National Guard Base in Iowa, not the damaged planes struck at Prince Sultan Air Base in Saudi Arabia. [sources]

**Why reject:** Rejecting only the photo — without affirming that the actual strike happened — implies the entire event is fabricated when it was real. Reject notes that debunk illustrative evidence (a mismatched photo or video) without clarifying that the underlying event they illustrate is confirmed; this silently denies the truth of a real event.

Reject notes that frame a clarification as a refutation and leave readers able to conclude the whole premise is a lie, when the accurate correction is more limited.

### Note leads with a concession that validates the tweet's framing

**Tweet:** Due to the Muslim population, German schools will no longer have musical education.  
[media: German news broadcast about Hamburg schools and religious intimidation]

**Note (REJECT):** While there are reports of religious intimidation in some Hamburg schools, including over music lessons deemed "haram" by some students, German schools are not eliminating music education. Officials confirm no ban is planned and such behavior won't be tolerated. [sources]

**Why reject:** The opening concession — "reports of religious intimidation... including over music lessons deemed haram" — restates and partially validates the tweet's framing before correcting it. Reject notes whose structure buries the correction after a concession that lets the core misinformation breathe; the correction must lead.

### Note omits the mechanism that makes the conspiracy implausible

**Tweet:** Sweet Angel Child Care which receives $1.26M in CCAP funding, their phone number goes DIRECTLY to the office of Tim Walz. Prosecute Tim Walz and the fraudsters.  
[media: video of man calling the number, reaching Walz constituent line]

**Note (REJECT):** The phone number listed for Sweet Angel Child Care on Google Maps previously dialed the MN Governor's office due to an erroneous listing, but the business's official number is (612) 721-1133. [sources]

**Why reject:** The note asserts a different number exists but does not explain how the wrong number ended up on Google Maps (user-editable or auto-scraped from a state licensing page listing the regulator's contact number). Without the mechanism, the bare contradiction is unconvincing and the conspiracy implication — a direct link between the daycare and the Governor — survives. Reject notes that state a counter-fact without the causal explanation that makes the original claim implausible.

### Note disputes credentials while missing the tweet's categorical overstatement

**Tweet:** Two economists just published a mathematical proof that AI will destroy the economy. Not might. Not could. Will — if nothing changes. The paper is called "The AI Layoff Trap." Wharton School. Boston University. Peer reviewed.

**Note (REJECT):** The paper cited is a non-peer-reviewed preprint. It models a theoretical scenario where AI automation could lead to economic issues under specific assumptions. Co-author Falk specializes in computer science, not economics. [sources]

**Why reject:** The tweet's core manipulation is upgrading a conditional theoretical result ("if AI displaces workers faster than...") to a certainty ("Will — not might, not could"). The note attacks credentials instead of making that distinction explicit. Reject notes that focus on process or credential critiques when the real harm is a categorical overstatement that the note leaves entirely unaddressed.
