# REJECT Exemplars: Sourcing Defects and Miscellaneous Note Quality Issues

These examples teach the judge to reject notes that fail on sourcing or note-quality grounds, independent of whether the underlying correction is directionally correct. The recurring lesson: a note's sources must actually substantiate the specific claim it makes — a true conclusion delivered on anonymous testimony, a Grok link, a topic-index page, or a citation that simply does not match the stated fact is still a bad note. Separately, notes aimed at obvious satire, parody-labeled content, or tweets that make no concrete factual assertion should be rejected because they add no useful context, not because the correction is wrong.

## Sourcing Defects

### Unsourced assertion — all citations are anonymous social-media posts

**Tweet:** "The level of AI slopification is so high that Ariana Grande has 6 fingers on the cover of Vogue Japan and NOBODY noticed it before publishing it" [media: portrait cover, Vogue Japan Mar 2024 masthead visible]
**Note (REJECT):** Ariana Grande's Vogue Japan photoshoot image shows an extra finger due to a Photoshop compositing error (common in blending photos), not AI generation. Fabric patterns are consistent and no other typical AI artifacts appear. https://x.com/Pentagear/... https://x.com/MOZZIEMUTANT/... https://x.com/billybinion/...
**Why reject:** Reject notes whose only evidence is a handful of anonymous X posts. Anonymous social-media agreement is not a verifiable source; readers cannot independently confirm the claim.

### Sourced to a non-authoritative AI tool (Grok link)

**Tweet:** "Btw if anyone with Ray-Ban glasses is recording you, just pull up a picture of the ISIS flag on your phone and hold it up to their glasses. It'll permanently ban their account almost immediately"
**Note (REJECT):** Showing an ISIS flag to Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses won't instantly ban the wearer's account… https://x.com/grok/status/... https://about.fb.com/news/2018/04/community-standards-examples
**Why reject:** Reject notes that cite a Grok response or an AI-generated summary as a primary source. AI output is not independently verifiable evidence, and a 2018 generic policy blog post does not address the specific technical claim about glasses account-banning.

### Source doesn't prove the specific claim (government event page that doesn't verify the assertion)

**Tweet:** "Netanyahu is certainly dead on March 9, his son observed 7 days of shiva then resumed posting exactly 7 days later."
**Note (REJECT):** Benjamin Netanyahu is alive and visited Ashdod Port on March 10, 2026, after the claimed date of death. https://www.gov.il/en/pages/event-ashdod100326 https://www.yahoo.com/...
**Why reject:** Reject notes where the lead citation is a government event-listing page that may be inaccessible or not self-evidently prove live presence — skeptical readers will challenge it. Prefer corroborated news footage or press conference citations over a single event-page link for extraordinary claims like death hoaxes.
**X crowd:** CURRENTLY_RATED_NOT_HELPFUL, 108 ratings

### Topic-index page instead of a dedicated fact-check

**Tweet:** [Image meme] "Republicans are trying to rig elections by only allowing U.S. citizens to vote!" — attributed to AOC with no satire label
**Note (REJECT):** This is not a real quote from Rep. Ocasio-Cortez. Searches of her official X account return no such statement, and fabricated quotes attributed to her are common. https://x.com/AOC https://www.factcheck.org/person/alexandria-ocasio-cortez/
**Why reject:** Reject notes that link to a public figure's profile page and a journalist's general topic index instead of a specific article debunking this exact fabricated quote. The note's claims are unverified by the provided links.

## Irrelevant Sources (Citation Doesn't Support the Headline Claim)

### Note identifies recycled footage but cites sources that don't prove the origin

**Tweet:** "Here is an Iranian missile exploding near US soldiers at Prince Sultan Air Base in Saudi Arabia." [media: grainy night-sky video]
**Note (REJECT):** The video shows Iran's 2020 missile attack on Al Asad Air Base in Iraq, not a recent strike on Prince Sultan Air Base in Saudi Arabia… https://www.dvidshub.net/video/785331/al-asad-tbm-attack …
**Why reject:** Reject notes that make a confident specific-origin identification ("this is the 2020 Al Asad video") and then cite a source that does not visually or textually match that precise clip. The sourcing must directly verify the specific provenance claim, not just establish that related events occurred.
**X crowd:** CURRENTLY_RATED_NOT_HELPFUL, 49 ratings

### Note claims recycled footage but cited links cover a different event entirely

**Tweet:** "It's raining hell in Tel Aviv and there isn't ONE interceptor missile in sight!" [media: nighttime missile/explosion footage]
**Note (REJECT):** The video is recycled from Iran's 2024 missile attack on Nevatim airbase, not current 2026 strikes on Tel Aviv… https://www.aljazeera.com/... https://www.reuters.com/... https://edition.cnn.com/...
**Why reject:** Reject notes that assert a video is archival footage from a named past event when all cited sources cover only the current event — the origin claim is the note's main contribution, and none of the links support it.
**X crowd:** CURRENTLY_RATED_NOT_HELPFUL, 9 ratings

## Note Not Needed

### Reaction tweet with no factual assertion — note corrects a claim nobody made

**Tweet:** "New fear unlocked: 😨" [video: cinematic water-park scene from a 2021 Polish thriller]
**Note (REJECT):** Misleading — the clip is a scripted scene from the 2021 Polish thriller Small World (Światło), shown in the film's trailer and documented on IMDb…
**Why reject:** Reject notes that correct an implied interpretation no reasonable reader would take as a sincere factual claim. A pure reaction emoji tweet invites viewers to enjoy the clip's tone; adding a "this is fiction" correction is unnecessary context that adds noise without preventing real misinformation.
**X crowd:** CURRENTLY_RATED_NOT_HELPFUL, 21 ratings

### Note fact-checks obvious AI-generated political satire

**Tweet:** "Trump makes a major announcement about the war with Iran" [video: AI-generated cartoon of Trump and Netanyahu with absurd Epstein-files dialogue]
**Note (REJECT):** The video is manipulated with fake audio depicting nonexistent dialogue about Epstein files; Trump announced military action against Iran in a separate Truth Social video, not on an aircraft carrier.
**Why reject:** Reject notes that treat unambiguously satirical AI-generated content as a factual claim requiring rebuttal. When the visuals are clearly exaggerated cartoon AI and the dialogue is transparently absurd, a note adds no useful correction and will be seen as missing the point.

### Note is redundant with the account's own parody disclosure

**Tweet:** "Timothée Chalamet is under fire for calling Helen Keller 'not real'" — posted by @DailyNoud (bio: "PARODY Hip-Hop News | These are fake stories")
**Note (REJECT):** This post from parody account @DailyNoud fabricates a quote attributed to Timothée Chalamet; the account's bio states "These are fake stories."
**Why reject:** Reject notes whose sole value is pointing to a disclosure that is already visible on the account itself. If the note's entire substance is "the bio says parody," the note restates what X already surfaces and provides no additional context.

## Hard to Understand / Poorly Constructed

### Note leads by partially validating the misleading framing it should rebut

**Tweet:** "In 1994, Japan ceased all mandatory vaccines for children under 24 months old. Can you guess what happened next?" [video overlaying text claiming Japan's low infant mortality proves anti-vax policy works]
**Note (REJECT):** Japan did end vaccine mandates in 1994, making them recommended. For context, infants under 2 still receive routine vaccines with >95% coverage. Infant mortality declined steadily before 1994; no link to policy change…
**Why reject:** Reject notes that open by conceding the misleading premise ("Japan did end vaccine mandates in 1994") before burying the rebuttal. A note that leads with the false framing risks reinforcing it; the correction must come first and be unambiguous.
**X crowd:** NEEDS_MORE_RATINGS, 67 ratings

### Note editorializes on an adjacent fan debate instead of stating the core fact

**Tweet:** 🚨 "Jurgen Klopp: 'Cristiano has achieved everything, he can retire anytime. Messi still has a lot to win in football.'"
**Note (REJECT):** This alleged quote from Jürgen Klopp is fabricated, as no credible sources confirm it. Klopp has actually said Messi is better than Ronaldo and the best he's seen. [Sources: UPI, YouTube, Goal.com]
**Why reject:** Reject notes that append an editorial opinion ("Messi is better than Ronaldo") to what should be a neutral debunking. Including a charged fan-debate aside makes the note appear partisan and distracts from the one relevant fact: the quote was fabricated.
**X crowd:** NEEDS_MORE_RATINGS, 81 ratings
