# No Note Needed — Reject Catalog

A note is the wrong tool for most tweets. The 235 `needs_note = NO` examples below cluster into nine recurring failure modes, ranked by approximate frequency. The core transfer principle: **a note's job is to correct a false factual belief a reader might form — not to police tone, label the obvious, resolve contested opinions, or repeat what the tweet already said.** Each section below gives 1–2 clean REJECT exemplars a judge can use as few-shot anchors.

**Ranked recurring reasons a note isn't needed (approximate counts):**

1. **Opinion / subjective / unfalsifiable claim** — ~75 rows. Tweet states a viewpoint, legal interpretation, rhetorical framing, or vague generalization with no discrete false fact to correct.
2. **Tweet is accurate, note is wrong** — ~70 rows. The note flatly contradicts a claim the tweet got right; the note itself is the misinformation.
3. **Joke / satire / obvious hyperbole** — ~65 rows. Audience universally reads it as humor; earnest fact-checking the punchline makes the note look foolish.
4. **Trivial pedantry** — ~50 rows. Note "corrects" a minor detail (wrong year, wrong agency, rounding error) that doesn't change the tweet's meaning or mislead readers.
5. **Note confirms / repeats the tweet** — ~20 rows. The note adds no correction; it restates or slightly elaborates what the tweet already said.
6. **Note addresses a claim the tweet never made** — ~18 rows. Strawman: note reacts to an implication or a different framing, not the actual text.
7. **Personal anecdote / unverifiable statement** — ~15 rows. First-person or private-individual claim that community notes cannot verify or falsify.
8. **Self-evident AI/fiction (redundant labeling)** — ~12 rows. The post is already labeled, visually obvious, or universally recognized as non-real; a note adds nothing.
9. **Note injects one side of a contested narrative** — ~10 rows. On genuinely disputed events, the note picks the official/opposing account and presents it as settled fact.

## 1. Joke / Satire / Obvious Hyperbole (~65)

### example A
**Tweet:** "YouTuber Markiplier broke down in tears after his self-financed horror movie 'Iron Lung' opened to $23 at the worldwide box office: 'Fuuuck dude this movie cost so much money I'm so fucked'"
**Note (REJECT — no note needed):** This is false. "Iron Lung" opened to $21.7 million worldwide, not $23. The quote is fabricated — the film was a major success, quadrupling its budget in one day.
**Why no note:** The $23 figure is deliberately absurd and the quote is transparently fabricated comedy; 191K likes and the top reply ("This is real he told me") signal the audience is entirely in on the joke. Correcting a punchline with real box-office figures does not reduce confusion — the humor depends on the audience already knowing the film succeeded.

### example B

**Why no note:** The photo is immediately recognizable to any audience familiar with Live Aid; the caption is sarcastic mockery of protest-crowd hype, not a sincere mislabeling. When every top reply plays along with the joke, a clarifying note adds nothing and signals the judge failed to read tone.

## 9. Note Injects One Side of a Contested Narrative (~10)

### example A

**Note (REJECT — no note needed):** Russia denies launching missiles at Kharkiv on January 2, 2026, claiming the blast resulted from a detonation of Ukrainian ammunition stored in a disused shopping center.
**Why no note:** Independent outlets (Reuters, Euronews, Kyiv Independent) confirm Russian Iskander missiles struck Kharkiv on Jan 2, 2026, killing at least three civilians. The note launders a Russian denial onto accurate, corroborated reporting. Where one side's denial contradicts independently verified facts, amplifying that denial is not neutral context — it is partisan counter-narrative injection.
**X crowd:** CURRENTLY_RATED_NOT_HELPFUL, 35 ratings

### example B
**Tweet:** "An anti-ICE protester alleges he was struck by a Tennessee Highway Patrol vehicle during an anti-ICE protest. Here is the dash cam footage:" [THP dashcam video]
**Note (REJECT — no note needed):** The Tennessee Highway Patrol states the protester was not struck by the vehicle, as dash cam video shows him holding onto it, lying down, then standing and walking away unharmed.
**Why no note:** The tweet itself uses hedged language ("alleges") and presents the dashcam as the evidence. The note treats THP's own official statement as settled fact in a genuinely contested incident where the protester disputes what happened. Presenting one party's account as the correction to the other party's allegation is framing, not fact-checking.
**X crowd:** CURRENTLY_RATED_NOT_HELPFUL, 145 ratings
