# Brief: AI model training emissions

Source: https://blog.andymasley.com/p/training-ai-models-doesnt-emit-that

A post is misleading on this topic if it presents the CO2 emissions of *training* an AI model as a uniquely catastrophic climate problem — typically by comparing one model's training to an individual's actions (a person's yearly emissions, a single flight, a car's lifetime) without dividing by the model's hundreds of millions of users.

Key debunking facts:
- The viral "training an AI model = 626,000 lbs CO2 (~5 cars' lifetimes)" chart is a single old 2019 model, predating commercial chatbots, miscited years later as if it represents modern AI.
- A high-end estimate of GPT-4's training (including failed runs) is ~15,000 tonnes CO2. The most extreme all-in estimate (embodied hardware + data-center construction) is still only ~30,000 tonnes.
- Fair comparison is to creating other mass-market products: training GPT-4 ≈ manufacturing all CDs for GTA5 (~13,500 t), less than a month of Lego sets (~25,000 t), less than a day of Nike shoes (~30,000 t) or iPhones (~33,000 t), far below a day of Coke cans (~82,500 t) or one cruise ship (~100,000 t).
- Manufacturing all iPhone 16s emits ~12 million tonnes — roughly 800x GPT-4's training. Per user, training GPT-4 costs about the emissions of one CD.

A post is NOT misleading for discussing aggregate training across *all* AI models as a growing share of US electricity (a legitimate infrastructure topic), or for stating accurate training figures without the catastrophizing framing.
