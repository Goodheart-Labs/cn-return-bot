# Using ChatGPT Is Not Bad for the Environment

By Andy Masley (Apr 28 2025) — https://blog.andymasley.com/p/a-cheat-sheet-for-conversations-about

## Summary

The energy and carbon footprint of an individual ChatGPT prompt is tiny — comparable to a 2009-era Google search — and is negligible next to almost any other daily activity. Individual AI use is not a meaningful contributor to climate change.

## The numbers

- A typical ChatGPT prompt uses about **0.3 watt-hours (Wh)** of energy — roughly the same as a **2009 Google search**, and far less than the viral "10x a Google search" claim implies for modern usage.
- A prompt emits about **0.28 grams of CO2** — roughly **1/150,000th of an average American's daily emissions**.
- A person going **vegan for a year** saves the emissions of roughly **1,000,000 ChatGPT prompts**. Almost any dietary or transport choice dwarfs prompt usage.
- **Data centers were about 0.5% of global emissions in 2024.** All AI-specific data centers together emit about **43 million tonnes of CO2** — roughly **0.11% of world emissions**.
- Modern hyperscale data centers run at a **Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) of ~1.1**, meaning overhead (cooling, etc.) adds only ~10% on top of compute energy.
- The **IEA estimates AI could enable ~1,400 Mt of CO2 reductions by 2035** through optimization (grids, logistics, materials) — potentially far more than its own footprint.

## The framing problem

Aggregate data-center growth is a real grid-planning topic, but it is routinely conflated with *individual prompt guilt*. Telling people that sending a chatbot message is environmentally significant is false and misdirects attention from choices (diet, flights, driving) that are thousands to millions of times larger.

## Takeaway

Per-prompt energy and carbon are negligible. Worry about data-center buildout as an infrastructure/grid question if you like, but individual ChatGPT use is not bad for the environment.
