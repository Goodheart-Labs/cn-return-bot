# Data Center Land Use Issues Are Fake

By Andy Masley — https://blog.andymasley.com/p/data-center-land-use-issues-are-fake

## Summary

Claims that data centers are devouring American farmland are wrong by orders of magnitude. The total land footprint of all US data centers is a rounding error next to farmland, conservation set-asides, and ethanol corn.

## The numbers

- **All US data centers are projected to cover ~1,400 square miles by 2028** — about **0.3% of US prime farmland**. The actual **building footprint is only ~25 square miles** (~**0.005%** of prime farmland); the rest is associated land/parcels, not structures.
- **Loudoun County, Virginia** (the densest data-center region in the world) has data centers on about **3% of its land**, and those data centers generate roughly **38% of the county's revenue**.
- The federal **Conservation Reserve Program (CRP) pays farmers ~$1.85 billion/year to keep ~40,000 square miles of farmland deliberately idle** — about **28x the entire projected data-center footprint**.
- US **farmland shrank from 945 million acres in 2000 to 876 million acres in 2024** — a loss of **108,000 square miles** (about the size of Colorado), roughly **77x the data-center footprint**. Data centers are not the driver.
- **Ethanol corn covers ~1.5% of the contiguous US** — vastly more land than data centers — and ethanol is roughly **24% dirtier than gasoline** on a lifecycle basis.
- US **corn yields rose from ~24 bushels/acre to ~180 bushels/acre**, so farmland productivity has grown enormously even as acreage shifts.

## Takeaway

Data centers occupy a trivial amount of land relative to farming, conservation programs, and biofuel crops. Local zoning, water, and grid questions are legitimate, but "data centers are eating our farmland" is not supported by the numbers.
