# The AI Water Issue Is Fake

By Andy Masley — https://blog.andymasley.com/p/the-ai-water-issue-is-fake

## Summary

Claims that using AI (e.g. ChatGPT) meaningfully harms water supplies are wrong by orders of magnitude. AI's total water footprint is a tiny fraction of US water use, and the per-prompt figures that go viral are inflated by 50–250x.

## The numbers

- US data centers used roughly **200–250 million gallons/day** in 2023 counting both onsite cooling and the offsite water used to generate their electricity. **Onsite water use alone was about 50 million gallons/day** — roughly **0.04% of US freshwater withdrawals**, and only about **3% of what the US golf industry uses**.
- Counting offsite (electricity-generation) water, AI/data centers are still only about **0.04% of US freshwater**; onsite-only is closer to **0.008%**.
- A single ChatGPT-style prompt uses on the order of **2 mL of water including offsite**, or about **0.3 mL onsite**. That is a fraction of a teaspoon.
- The **average American's daily water footprint is ~422 gallons**. That is equivalent to roughly **800,000 ChatGPT prompts**.
- Electricity generation uses about **4.35 liters of water per kWh**, which is why most of a data center's "water footprint" is actually offsite power-plant water, not the building itself.

## Concepts people conflate

- **Consumptive vs. non-consumptive use**: much water that passes through cooling or power generation is returned to the source, not consumed.
- **Direct (onsite) vs. indirect (offsite) use**: the scary totals usually fold in electricity-generation water and attribute it entirely to AI.
- **Potable vs. non-potable**: many data centers use reclaimed/non-potable water.

## The viral articles, debunked

- **NYT / Georgia reporting**: the water problems near a Georgia data center were tied to **construction sediment**, not the ongoing operation of the facility.
- **Washington Post "bottle of water per email"**: this figure was inflated by roughly **50–250x**. It bundled offsite electricity water and worst-case assumptions and presented them as typical.
- **Amazon Oregon nitrate case**: Amazon was **1 of 17 defendants**; it settled to avoid litigation. The **nitrate contamination came from agricultural runoff (farms)**, not the data center.

## Takeaway

Individual AI use has a negligible water footprint. The honest framing is that data center water use is a small, local, and largely solvable infrastructure question — not a reason for individuals to feel guilty about sending prompts.
