# Five Unresolved Issues in OpenAI's Deal With the Department of Defense

By Jake Laperruque (Mar 9 2026) — https://www.techpolicy.press/five-unresolved-issues-in-openais-deal-with-the-department-of-defense/

## Background

In February 2026 the Department of Defense designated **Anthropic a "supply chain risk."** Around the same time **OpenAI announced a deal with the DoD**, accompanied by three public "red lines":

1. No mass domestic surveillance.
2. No directing autonomous weapons.
3. No high-stakes automated decisions.

The piece argues these commitments, while welcome, leave **five issues unresolved**.

## The five unresolved issues

1. **"Mass domestic surveillance" is undefined.** Without a definition tied to existing law (e.g. **Executive Order 12333**, **FISA**), the term can be read narrowly enough to permit large programs that the public would consider mass surveillance.

2. **"Intentional"/"deliberate" loopholes.** Qualifiers like *intentional* or *deliberate* create gaps: a system can cause sweeping effects without "intending" to. The author cites a hypothetical **Muslim prayer app with ~98 million users** to illustrate how incidental data could enable de facto surveillance of a group without anyone "intending" mass surveillance.

3. **Tracking vs. surveillance ambiguity.** The red lines don't clearly cover location tracking and other monitoring that fall short of the narrow "surveillance" label.

4. **Legal rationales without public review.** Past abuses (e.g. **OLC torture memos**, pre-**Carpenter v. United States (2018)** location-data theories) show that secret legal interpretations can authorize practices the public never gets to scrutinize.

5. **Intelligence-agency exclusion may not be durable.** Even if intelligence agencies are excluded now, nothing guarantees that exclusion persists, and the boundary between DoD and IC use can erode.

## Context references

- The Israeli **"Lavender"** target-selection system is cited as a real-world example of AI in lethal targeting.
- **Caribbean boat strikes** are referenced as an example of contested lethal operations.

## Takeaway

The story is nuanced: OpenAI's red lines are real commitments, but the article's point is that key terms are **undefined or loophole-prone**, and oversight mechanisms are unproven — not that OpenAI is "arming killer robots." Notes on posts about this deal should reflect that nuance rather than either extreme.
