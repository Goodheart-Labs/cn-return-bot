Privacy Policy

Common Notes (commonnotes.net and the Common Notes browser extension) · Last updated 18 August 2026

What we collect

  • Reading: no account needed, no tracking cookies, no third-party trackers. We count usage ourselves: the website records events such as page views in our own database, under a random identifier stored in your browser. The identifier is not derived from anything about you, and it is replaced with a fresh one when you sign out.
  • Signing in with email: your email address, used only to send a 6-digit login code.
  • Signing in with X: your X handle, display name, and the email X shares with us.
  • Contributing: your votes, suggested improvements, and draft notes, linked to your account. Draft notes display your handle or display name publicly. When you rate a note, we record the charity you pick for the donation we pledge on that rating (the donation is our money, not yours). While you are signed in, usage events also carry your account id.

The browser extension

The Common Notes browser extension shows notes directly on the pages they were written about. It asks for access to all websites at install, because notes can exist anywhere — but it is built to know as little about your browsing as possible:

  • Matching happens on your device. The extension periodically downloads the list of pages that have notes and checks the pages you visit against that local list. Browsing a page without notes does not make the extension look anything up on our servers.
  • Pages with notes: to display notes, the page's address is sent to our server (together with your login token if you are signed in). The same happens for the page you are on when you open the extension's toolbar popup.
  • Visit counting on three sites, if you allow it: on Substack, YouTube, and LessWrong, opening a post or video records an anonymous visit in our database: the page's address and the time, with no user or device identifier. This covers posts with and without notes; the counts tell us which pages our fact-checking pipeline should look at. It is controlled by one checkbox per site, all shown on the extension's settings page that opens once after install. Nothing is recorded before that page has been shown, and unticking a site stops recording there immediately.
  • Contributing from the extension: "Request Common Notes" stores the page's address, title, and text (and the paragraph you selected, if any), so our pipeline can fact-check pages it cannot download itself. Requesting notes on all new posts from an author stores that author's feed address. Writing a note stores the text you selected, your note, and the page's address and title.
  • Moderation: before a note or improvement is posted, its text and the selected passage are checked by an AI model via OpenRouter (Anthropic) to filter out non-earnest submissions. The check returns only accept or reject and stores nothing; no name, email, or page address is included.
  • Settings: small preferences (the visit-counting checkboxes, which overlays to show, which notes to display, which pages and authors you already requested notes for) live in your browser's built-in extension sync storage — they travel with your browser account (e.g. Chrome sync), not through our servers. The extension also remembers locally which offer messages it already showed you, so it does not repeat them.
  • Usage analytics, self-hosted: the extension records a few usage events in our own database — that it was installed, that notes were shown on a page, that a sign-in was started, that a submission was rejected by moderation. Events carry a random device identifier (replaced with a fresh one when you sign out) and, while you are signed in, your account id. No third-party analytics, advertising, or tracking code is involved. Uninstalling the extension removes everything it stored in your browser.

Where it lives

Account data and contributions are stored with Supabase (our database provider). Login emails are delivered via Resend. The site is served through Cloudflare. The moderation check for submitted notes runs through OpenRouter (Anthropic). We do not sell your data or use third-party advertising or analytics trackers.

Cookies

Only what sign-in requires: a session token in your browser's local storage (for the extension, in its own extension storage). No advertising or cross-site tracking.

Your choices

  • You can delete your own draft notes in the interface.
  • To delete your account and its data, email us and we'll do it.
